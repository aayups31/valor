A simulated service workflow exercises it outside robotics. The rover remains one benchmark. ## How a decision works

1. A task adapter supplies the public state, current generation, available resources, remaining time and explicit action/continuation candidates. Forecasts cannot use the actual future random event stream.
2. The core reads the external policy and bounded forecast budget. Under low slack it checks shorter task candidates first; the policy itself is unchanged.
3. Each forecast must match the observation, generation, candidate, continuation, domain and sufficient horizon. Missing quantities, conflicting outcome probabilities or unsupported evidence cause rejection rather than optimistic substitution.
4. For supported forecasts, the core checks failure bounds, action plus recovery costs and reserves, projected capability and duration. It compares admissible plans using one objective:

   `task_value × success_probability − damage_cost × recoverable_damage − failure_cost × irreversible_failure_probability − time_cost × duration`

5. The result is an inspectable proposal or abstention. The final authority gate checks stop, state generation and deadline again before a short local application callback. Applying a proposal advances generation so it cannot be reused.
6. The benchmark separately records what actually happened. A forecast, proposed action, applied action and task outcome are different records.

Recoverable damage and irreversible failure have separate accounting so the same loss is not charged twice. Human policy may permit more machine risk; the core cannot increase that permission for itself. It has no operation for clearing an external stop. If an application callback raises after partial effects, the proposal is invalidated but those effects cannot be promised undone.

Evidence declarations currently come from trusted adapters. A production integration would need an approved-model registry, applicability/shift checks and task-specific action validation. This Python core is not a hard real-time executor, transaction manager or OS/robot safety boundary. The [general architecture](../01_Architecture/GENERAL_DECISION_CORE.md) records these limits.

## Learned threat development

### Inputs, labels and separation

The first encoder supplies ten public features: integrity, public load, remaining work, quota, available time, continuation duration and four continuation indicators. The generic learner imports no service or rover dynamics. Its first weights nevertheless apply only to this service encoder and continuation protocol.

Labels come from actual simulated whole-continuation outcomes. Irreversible outage is positive; completed, abandoned, deadline-missed and quota-depleted endings remain distinct known competing outcomes. Interrupted/unknown future windows are excluded instead of being labeled safe. Configuration and event randomness use independent streams so a public load cannot accidentally encode the next outage draw. Seeds, statuses, outcomes and analytic reference probabilities stay out of learned inputs. Analytic probabilities are used only for scoring reference.

| Role | Observed continuations | Failure events | Actual episode seeds |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Training | 6,000 | 225 | 0–5999 |
| Checkpoint selection | 800 | 41 | 10000–10799 |
| Separate development check | 800 | 34 | 12000–12799 |

The actual seed sets and file hashes were verified. There were no censored exclusions in these bounded collections. The 11000–11999 calibration range and 20000–29999 final-test range were not collected. All three datasets match the same synthetic collector distribution; seed separation does not establish policy/dynamics shift or real-world transfer.

### Models, comparator correction and work

The neural head has two 32-unit SiLU hidden layers and 1,441 parameters. The linear head has 11 parameters. Both use unweighted binary likelihood, the same public features, minibatch order, Adam learning rate, update opportunities and checkpoint-selection episodes. Parameter counts and computational work differ; equal FLOPs or wall time per model are not claimed.

Two development trials were retained, both with training seed 42. The first random initialization left the linear control worse than the constant-risk selection score after its short budget. Before scoring the separate check, the recipe was revised to initialize both output heads at the training failure rate, 3.75%, and make that constant prior an eligible epoch-0 checkpoint. Both models still perform actual training updates. This avoids presenting an unnecessarily weak initialization as an informative baseline. It is development tuning, not a locked experiment or an independent training replication.

Initialization, checkpoint I/O and completion of an operation are not hard-preempted. The second checkpoint was then scored once on the separate check episodes; the first was retained with selection results and was not scored on that set.

### Separate development check

Lower Brier and log loss are better probability scores. The constant predictor uses the training event rate. The analytic reference knows the benchmark dynamics and is not an equally informed learned competitor.

| Predictor | Brier | Log loss | Fixed-bin ECE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Neural | 0.036856 | 0.134733 | 0.007868 |
| Linear | 0.037780 | 0.146306 | 0.023118 |
| Constant training prevalence | 0.040719 | 0.176142 | 0.005000 |
| Analytic known-dynamics reference | 0.036570 | 0.131752 | 0.010540 |

The neural head improves these two proper scores over the simple learned controls on this development set. The difference from the linear head is modest, and there are only 34 failure events. The constant predictor's small ECE illustrates why an aggregate calibration statistic alone is insufficient: it cannot distinguish candidates.

Reliability is still imperfect. In the neural 0.10–0.20 prediction bin, 82 examples had mean predicted risk 0.137 but observed event rate 0.183. Bin averages do not provide an individual continuation's risk bound, and these sparse counts have no qualified threshold intervals. No independent calibration, shifted-policy acceptance or candidate-ranking gate has passed. The saved model and evaluation explicitly retain `evidence_kind: experimental` and `decision_ready: false`; they are not wired into the action permission mechanism.
