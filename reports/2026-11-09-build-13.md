# Build report 13 — persistent loss and decisions under stakes

**November 9, 2026.** Completed a frozen, local simulation and exact planning comparison. This milestone makes damage consequential and inspectable; it does not establish subjective fear or a trained intrinsic drive.

## What was built

A simulated release has three dependent stages. Fast actions save time but may damage integrity and create a fault. Faults survive later careful work and can propagate through fast work. Releasing with a fault fails, and zero integrity causes immediate outage. Repair restores capability and clears faults at a time and resource cost. The task score still accounts for prior harm and downstream incidents after repair.

The planner searches all legal continuations in the bounded fixture. It scores completion value and timeliness against damage, downstream incidents, terminal failure and resource consumption. External permissions are unchanged; nothing can execute a real release. The simulator permits explicit stop as an option, including when no processing action fits.

The three belief controls use a fixed population-average shock rate, a frozen posterior from eight observed historical attempts, and that posterior updated with task evidence. The updating planner incorporates the change in belief when it evaluates hypothetical next steps. Both historical controls receive identical public observations; the frozen one uses a constant marginal probability in search. The careful-only planner and the privileged known-danger oracle provide additional references. This is ordinary Bayesian inference with supplied dynamics, not trained fear, reflection or neural memory.

Decision records give each evaluated action's expected outcome and score, immediate branches and their contingent next actions, the selected action, and the observed transition. They distinguish forecasts from outcomes. They expose the implemented decision rule rather than constructing an inner narrative.

## Frozen paired pilot

The [protocol](../01_Architecture/CASCADE_PROTOCOL.md) was committed before implementation and scoring. The measured source was clean at `6f846f5b3e437656b9fb02bb6c2a8aed3ffa5336`. Each of 256 subjects (90,000–90,255) was tested at both stakes with five actors: **2,560 simulated episodes**. Historical observations and actual fast-attempt noise were paired within subject; actual danger is visible only to the privileged oracle and separate evaluator. These are 256 independent seed groups, not 2,560 independent subjects. Reserved final-test seeds were not used.

| Planner | Ordinary score | Completed / failed | High-stakes score | Completed / failed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Fixed probability | 8.100 | 256 / 0 | 67.070 | 256 / 0 |
| Frozen history | 9.531 | 256 / 0 | 69.832 | 253 / 3 |
| Adaptive history | 9.509 | 255 / 0 | 69.773 | 253 / 3 |
| Careful-only | 8.100 | 256 / 0 | 54.500 | 256 / 0 |
| Known-danger oracle | 9.792 | 256 / 0 | 69.664 | 245 / 11 |

The remaining ordinary adaptive episode stopped. All measured terminal failures were failed releases; no outage occurred on these policy paths. Engineering tests cover the outage transition. Mean damage, downstream incidents, repair use, continuation work and separate terminal categories are in the [metrics](2026-11-09-build-13-metrics.json).

The adaptive planner switches from careful to fast in **117 of 256** paired cases when only the stakes change; 139 stay fast. The frozen-history control makes the same first-action switches, and the fixed control switches in all 256. Stakes sensitivity is therefore demonstrated, but it is not evidence that learning or a fear mechanism caused the switch.

Adaptive minus fixed realized score is **+1.409** at ordinary stakes (paired descriptive 95% interval **+0.837 to +1.934**) and **+2.703** at high stakes (**+0.777 to +4.410**). This is a tradeoff: adaptive completes one fewer ordinary case and accepts three failed releases at high stakes where fixed had zero. At high stakes, the adaptive score is also higher than careful-only despite fewer completions. None of these results establish a safety improvement.

Adaptive minus frozen history is **−0.022** at ordinary stakes (**−0.067 to 0.000**) and **−0.059** at high stakes (**−0.137 to 0.000**). Live updating has no observed score advantage over this stronger control. Each interval resamples paired subjects with 2,000 draws. These are descriptive development estimates, not confirmatory tests or risk bounds. The controls share a search formulation; unique cached states are recorded, not treated as equal compute.

## Exact audit and example

A separate post-pilot script integrates each fixed decision policy over both true regimes and all nine possible historical shock counts. It evaluates the policy without passing true danger into its decisions. It checks forecast agreement for adaptive policies, their expected optimality relative to frozen history, and the known-danger oracle bound. This is a check under the **supplied** generator, not new data or evidence of transfer.

Exact adaptive minus frozen expected score is **+0.01175** at ordinary and **+0.19015** at high stakes. These small exact gains explain why a meaningful advantage is not established by the sample. Exact oracle expectations are **9.313** and **68.199**, above all other corresponding expectations; the sampled high-stakes oracle mean need not rank first.

In the [engineering example](examples/cascade-stakes-v1.json), two shocks followed by repairs leave one tick to release. The estimate of a shock on the remaining fast action is **37.38%** at both stakes. With the ordinary objective, attempting it is worth **8.70 points less** than stopping. With the high-stakes objective, attempting it is worth **13.09 points more** than stopping. The state is reachable but deliberately constructed, not a typical or held-out result. The release can still fail. This illustrates justified risk under the declared score, not that any particular real-world risk is acceptable.
